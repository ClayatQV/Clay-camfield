# Player Assessment — question bank (draft v1)

Status: **draft for Clay to mark up.** Nothing here is built yet.

The assessment is the backbone of the app. Its job: know each player well enough that the curriculum pushes them *just enough* — never too easy, never over their head — so they feel themselves getting better and keep coming back. The test of success: *"How does this thing know me so well?"*

---

## 1. Rules

1. **10 minutes max.** Target about 9½. If a section runs over its budget, it gets cut.
2. **Every question is one quick choice.** One question per screen, big buttons, choosing an answer moves you on (no "Next"). Back button and Skip are always there.
3. **Answer types:**
   - **Y/N** — Yes / No / Not sure. "Not sure" counts as No for placement.
   - **One** — pick one.
   - **Multi** — choose all that apply, then Done.
   - **Grid** — a grid of names or songs. **Select once = I know it, twice = I can play it** (select again to clear). One screen instead of 40 questions.
   - **Rank** — pick up to 3 in order.
   - Every list has **Other**.
4. **No self-ratings.** People rate themselves too high. We ask concrete things they can answer yes or no ("Do you know your major triads on the top 3 strings?").
5. **Adaptive like a placement test.** Each skill path is a ladder of rungs. Start in the middle; Yes jumps up, No steps down, stop at the boundary. The bank is deep; each person only sees about 10–15 questions per area.
6. **Know → Play → Use → Explain.** At the highest rung someone says they know, three quick follow-ups:
   - **Play:** "Can you play it on demand?"
   - **Use:** "Does it show up when you improvise or play songs?"
   - **Explain:** "Could you explain what you're playing and why?"

   Example: Clay on harmonic minor modes = Know ✓ Play ✓ Use ✗. That gap is exactly what the routine targets.
7. **Spot checks keep it honest.** A few questions have a right answer (marked ✓ below). If someone says Yes to a rung but misses its spot check, the app trusts their other Yes answers a little less (a "trust factor" per area). No audio clips; everything is questions.
8. **Depth follows knowledge.** In each genre, how many artists someone knows decides how deep that genre's questions go (§3.1).
9. **Influences and genres are signals, not limits.** If someone names Marcus King, the app may still send them Wes Montgomery. The job is the best route to what they want, not only the names they gave.

### 3.1 How deep each genre goes

The first grid in each genre module (artists) sets its depth:

| Artists known (of 20) | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0–4 | **Listening first.** Skip forms and applied theory; ask 1 forms question. The result says "start by listening" and fills the Listening tab. |
| 5–11 | Standard: forms path + feels + 1 applied question. |
| 12–20 | Full: everything, including the hardest applied theory. |

Knowing only the modern players but none of the roots (e.g., Kingfish and Gales but not B.B. or Albert King) is flagged as a **roots gap**.

### C. Forms — placement path (Y/N), start at F3

| Rung | Question |
|---|---|
| F1 | Can you play a 12-bar blues in any key? |
| F2 | Do you know the "quick change" (IV in bar 2)? ✓ *Spot check: "In a quick-change blues in A, bar 2 is…" → D7* |
| F3 | Can you play an 8-bar blues? |
| F4 | Can you play a minor blues? ✓ *"In a minor blues in A, bar 9 is usually…" → F7 (♭VI7)* |
| F5 | Can you play a jazz blues (I–IV–I–vi–ii–V, ii–V into IV)? |
| F6 | Can you play a Bird blues ("Blues for Alice" changes)? |

### D. Feels — Multi: "Choose the feels you can play on demand"

Shuffle · Texas shuffle · Slow blues in 12/8 · Chicago · Rumba blues · Delta/acoustic fingerpicking · Boogie · Jump blues/swing · Funky straight-16ths blues

### E. Turnarounds and must-have skills — placement path (Y/N), start at T2

| Rung | Question |
|---|---|
| T1 | Can you play a basic I–V turnaround? |
| T2 | Can you play the descending chromatic turnaround lick in E and A? |
| T3 | Can you play a I–VI–ii–V jazz turnaround? |
| T4 | Can you play a tritone-sub turnaround? |

Must-have skills — Multi: "Choose what you can do":
- Bends in tune (whole step)
- Controlled vibrato
- Call and response with a singer
- Fills behind a singer
- Mixing major and minor pentatonic with the chord
- Targeting the 3rd of the IV chord

### F. Applied theory — graded

| ID | Type | Question | Grading |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Multi | Over the **V chord in a blues turnaround**, what can you play? Choose all that work. | Minor pentatonic of the key (1) · V7 arpeggio (2) · Mixolydian of V (2) · Major pentatonic of V (2) · Half-whole diminished of V (3) · Altered of V (3) · "I just play the box" (0). **Level = highest weight picked**; follow-up Y/N: "Can you do that at tempo?" |
| A2 ✓ | One | In an A blues, A7 → D7: the 3rd of A7 (C♯) moves a half step to which note of D7? | Root · 3rd · 5th · **♭7 (C)** |
| A3 ✓ | One | Which note makes the classic B.B./Albert "major-minor" sound over A7? | **C (♭3) bending toward C♯ (3)** · E · G · F |

## 6. Section 3 — Skills placement (3 min)

Each area is a ladder. **Start** marks the first question asked; the Know → Play → Use → Explain follow-ups run only at the top rung reached. ✓ = spot check.

**Chords and voicings** (start C5)
1. C1 Open chords
2. C2 Barre chords (E and A shapes)
3. C3 7th chords: maj7, m7, 7
4. C4 Triads on the top 3 strings, all inversions
5. C5 Triads on every string set, all inversions
6. C6 Drop 2 voicings
7. C7 Drop 3 and shell voicings for comping
8. C8 Upper extensions: 9, 11, 13
9. C9 Altered dominants: 7♭9, 7♯9, 7alt
10. C10 Chord-melody (melody on top)

✓ *"A C major triad in 2nd inversion has which note on the bottom?" → G*

**Scales and modes** (start S4)
1. S1 Minor pentatonic, one position
2. S2 All 5 pentatonic positions
3. S3 Major scale across the whole neck
4. S4 Modes of the major scale (Dorian, Mixolydian…)
5. S5 Melodic minor and its modes (Lydian dominant, altered)
6. S6 Harmonic minor and its modes (Phrygian dominant…)
7. S7 Symmetric scales: diminished, whole tone

✓ *"Dorian is the major scale started from which degree?" → 2nd*

**Arpeggios** (start A3)
1. A1 Major and minor triad arpeggios
2. A2 7th arpeggios in one position
3. A3 7th arpeggios through the cycle of 4ths
4. A4 Connecting arpeggios through a ii–V–I
5. A5 Extended and altered arpeggios (9ths; diminished from the 3rd of V7)

**Fretboard** (start N2)
1. N1 Note names on the low E and A strings
2. N2 Any note on any string within about 3 seconds
3. N3 The CAGED system
4. N4 Find any chord tone (e.g. the 3rd) anywhere on the neck

✓ *"5th fret, G string?" → C*

**Theory** (start T3)
1. T1 Note names and scale degrees
2. T2 Cycle of 4ths and 5ths from memory
3. T3 Diatonic chords of any key and Nashville numbers
4. T4 ii–V–I in any key instantly
5. T5 Secondary dominants
6. T6 Tritone subs and modal interchange
7. T7 Negative harmony

✓ *"1–4–5 in G?" → G C D* · ✓ *"ii–V–I in B♭?" → Cm7 F7 B♭maj7*

**Ear** (start E3; Y/N only)
1. E1 Hear major vs. minor
2. E2 Learn a melody by ear
3. E3 Hear I–IV–V changes in a song
4. E4 Transcribe a solo by ear
5. E5 Hear chord colors (7♭9 vs. 7♯9, maj7 vs. 6)

**Improvising over changes** (start I2)
1. I1 One scale over a whole tune
2. I2 Change scales as the chords change
3. I3 Land on chord tones on beat 1
4. I4 Guide-tone lines (3rds and 7ths)
5. I5 Going outside and resolving on purpose

**Rhythm and comping** (start R3)
1. R1 Strumming patterns
2. R2 Rhythm parts learned from records
3. R3 Comping with 3–4 note voicings
4. R4 Voice-leading through changes
5. R5 Rhythmic variety: Freddie Green, Charleston, anticipations

**Technique** (Multi + One)
- *Multi — "Choose what you can do cleanly":* alternate picking · economy picking · legato · hybrid picking · sweep picking · tapping · string skipping · clean muting
- *One — "Fastest clean 16th notes?":* under 80 · 80–100 · 100–120 · 120+ bpm

**Timing and feel** (Multi — habits)
- Practice with a metronome
- Can put the click on 2 and 4
- Play with a live drummer weekly
- Swing vs. straight on demand
- 16th-note funk rhythm in the pocket
- Play behind or ahead of the beat on purpose
- Record yourself and listen back

*(Optional later: a play-along timing test.)*

**Vocabulary**
- *One — "How many licks can you play in any key?":* 0 · 1–5 · 6–20 · 20+
- *Y/N:* "Do the same licks keep coming out?" (Clay's core problem — a strong signal.)

**Reading** (Multi)
Tab · chord charts · Nashville number charts · lead sheets · standard notation (slowly) · sight-reading

**X1 vs. X2 matters:** a rusty former pro (Clay) needs a very different plan from a beginner who plays the same amount now.

**How playing context shapes the plan:**
- **Cover band** → learning songs fast, chord charts
- **Worship** → Nashville numbers, changing key on the fly
- **Session work** → reading, versatility
- **Touring sideman** → locking in with a band, knowing the catalog
- **Original artist** → writing your own parts and lines

## 9. Section 6 — Results

What the player sees:
1. **Player type:** home genre + role + profile shape. E.g. *"Feel-First Soul Player."*
2. **A short read:** strengths, the biggest gap between **Know** and **Use**, and the one area that would unlock the most.
3. **Map:** every area with its rung and Know/Play/Use/Explain state.
4. **Listening list:** the artists they don't know in their genres (roots first if there's a roots gap), each with a key album and a song.
5. **Their first routine.**

How the routine is built:
- **Warm-ups:** the next rung above the highest **Play** rung in chords and arpeggios. Short.
- **Concept:** the area with the biggest **Know but not Use** gap, weighted by G2 goals.
- **Application:** about half the session. 3–5 options:
  - 2–3 from their genres and influences
  - 1 from the target genre
  - 1 discovery pick
- **Minutes:** from G4.

**Retake after each block:** about 3 minutes. Re-run placement only for the areas that block trained, plus G2 ("What do you want next?").
